# Mission Reflection

Completing this mission helped me understand the role of cloud computing and how different cloud platforms can be used to meet business needs. Among the three major cloud platforms, **Amazon Web Services (AWS)** impressed me the most because of its wide range of services, global infrastructure, and flexibility. I found AWS interesting because it provides many tools that can support different types of organizations, from startups to large enterprises.

AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform (GCP) have many similarities. All three provide cloud services such as computing, storage, databases, networking, security, and monitoring. They also support scalability, allowing businesses to increase or decrease resources depending on their needs. Another similarity is that they use a pay-as-you-go model for many services, which can help organizations manage their expenses.

Business requirements strongly influence the selection of a cloud platform. Companies need to consider factors such as budget, performance, security, scalability, existing systems, technical skills, and the type of applications they will run. A startup with a limited budget may prioritize affordable and easy-to-use services, while a large company may prioritize security, reliability, compliance, and advanced services.

Acting as a **Cloud Solutions Consultant** taught me that choosing a cloud platform is not simply about selecting the most popular provider. It requires understanding the client's goals and matching their requirements with the appropriate cloud services. I learned to compare platforms and make recommendations based on business needs.

Finally, this mission improved my **GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio** by allowing me to organize my research, comparisons, recommendations, and reflections in a structured way. It also helped me improve my Markdown, documentation, GitHub, and cloud computing skills. Overall, this mission gave me a better understanding of how cloud technology can support real-world organizations.
